# CivicSense implementation outcomes

## 1. Public civic landing and shared accessible product shell
Create a professional public landing page with CivicSense logo, Home, Report Issue, Track Complaint, Nearby Issues, Safety, Awareness, About, Login, and Register navigation; primary CTA “Report a Civic Issue”; secondary CTA “Track Complaint”; hero headline “Your Voice. Your City. Your CivicSense.”; subheading “Report civic problems, connect with the right authority, and track every step until resolution.”; Report an Issue and Track My Complaint CTAs; a serious civic-themed map/issue visual; a five-step Report/Verify/Route/Track/Resolve explanation; category cards for Roads & Potholes, Streetlights, Garbage & Sanitation, Water & Drainage, Traffic & Signals, Public Infrastructure, Environment, Public Safety, Women’s Safety, and Other; responsive mobile-first layout; keyboard navigation; proper labels; strong contrast; visible focus states; accessible buttons; loading, empty, network-error, unauthorized, and server-error states. Avoid excessive gradients, neon colors, cartoon graphics, glassmorphism, and generic AI-looking visuals.

## 2. Role-aware authentication and application access
Use real Manus OAuth/application sessions with the starter’s `webdev_app_session` cookie and authenticated user records. Provide role-based access for Citizen, Authority Officer, Higher Authority, and System Admin. Citizens can register/login and officers, higher authorities, and admins can access only their authorized work surfaces. Do not invent a development bypass or store passwords in plain text. Demo accounts include citizen@demo.com, officer@demo.com, and admin@demo.com, with the demo password/authentication expectations documented rather than hard-coded into production code.

## 3. Complaint data model, deterministic classification, routing, and REST APIs
Persist users, roles, departments, authorities, issue categories, complaints, complaint media, complaint locations, status history, assignments, comments, SLA rules, escalation rules, escalations, notifications, safety reports, and audit logs with proper relationships, indexes, and timestamps. Implement REST APIs: POST /api/auth/register, POST /api/auth/login, POST /api/auth/logout; POST/GET /api/complaints, GET/PATCH /api/complaints/:id, POST /api/complaints/:id/comments, /assign, /resolve, and /escalate; GET /api/categories; GET /api/departments; GET /api/notifications; POST/GET /api/safety-reports; and GET /api/analytics/dashboard. Validate inputs and return friendly errors. Use deterministic classification from category, description, location, and severity; store configurable routing rules in the backend rather than hard-coding them throughout the frontend; do not claim fake AI functionality.

## 4. Citizen report, tracking, dashboard, and nearby issues workflow
Implement a multi-step report form with Issue (category, title, description, severity, photo, video), Location (current location, search, interactive map, latitude/longitude, readable address), Details (noticed date, dangerous flag, current-risk flag, additional comments), and Review steps. Submit Complaint must create a persisted complaint, generate an ID such as CIV-2026-000124, and show success with complaint ID, category, location, department, current status, estimated resolution time, and next step. Implement tracking by complaint ID and authenticated mobile/email context with a visual timeline for Submitted → Verified → Assigned → In Progress → Resolved → Citizen Confirmation → Closed, including timestamp, status, department, action, and officer note. Citizen dashboard shows total/open/in-progress/resolved/escalated complaints, complaint cards/table with SLA countdown, recent activity, and nearby issues. Nearby issues map markers show category, severity, status, and report date with category/severity/status/distance filters and issue previews.

## 5. SLA, lifecycle updates, comments, resolution confirmation, notifications, and escalation
Implement real status transitions through report, validate, classify, route, assign, investigate, resolve, verify, close; allow comments/additional information, assignments, notes, resolution proof metadata, resolve, citizen confirmation, reopen, and notifications. Configure SLA values for low (5 days), medium (3 days), high (48 hours), and critical (24 hours) priority, including category overrides where configured. When an SLA expires without resolution, create a real escalation record and show that the complaint exceeded its deadline and has been escalated through the configured higher authority path. Create in-app notifications for submission, verification, assignment, status changes, officer comments, approaching SLA deadline, escalation, resolution, and citizen confirmation. Keep an abstraction for future email/SMS/push delivery.

## 6. Authority officer operations dashboard
Create a separate authority dashboard showing total assigned, new, in progress, resolved, overdue, and escalated complaints; charts for complaints by category/status, resolution time, SLA compliance, and area; and a complaint table with ID, category, location, priority, submitted date, SLA, status, assigned officer, and actions. Officers can filter by category/location/priority/status; open complaint details; see photos/location; accept/assign; change status; add investigation notes; upload resolution proof; communicate with citizens; mark resolved; view SLA/deadline; see overdue complaints; and escalate when required. Each action must update persistence, history, notifications, and audit data rather than being a no-op.

## 7. Higher-authority oversight and admin controls
Higher authorities can view escalated, overdue, SLA-violating, and unresolved complaints; review escalation history; reassign complaints; monitor department performance; view resolution trends/geographic hotspots; generate reports; and filter by department, area, category, date, and priority. Admins can manage users, departments (add/edit/disable), issue categories and routing, SLA rules, escalation rules, reports including complaint statistics/resolution rate/average resolution/SLA compliance, audit logs, emergency resources, and platform settings. Admin-only changes must be protected and auditable.

## 8. Responsible Women’s Safety module and privacy boundaries
Provide a dedicated Safety section with configurable emergency contact placeholders that do not pretend to provide live emergency response; a safety map for poorly lit areas, unsafe public locations, harassment concerns, abandoned areas, and other safety concerns; a private safety report flow; and a safe-route prototype with From → To inputs and a clear prototype label when actual safety scoring/routing is not implemented. Do not expose sensitive personal information publicly. Safety reports and any media must have stricter privacy controls and role-aware access.

## 9. Demo data, documentation, and future-AI boundaries
Seed realistic sample citizens, authority officers, higher authority/admin users, departments, categories, complaints, locations, status histories, notifications, escalated/overdue complaints, and safety reports so dashboards are populated after setup; clearly label sample data. Provide README.md and feature documentation covering problem statement, solution, architecture diagram, database schema, API documentation, setup, environment variables, demo accounts, screenshots guidance, future improvements, storage/map assumptions, and the exact integration points for optional future image classification, duplicate detection, priority prediction, routing, trend/hotspot detection, resolution prediction, and natural-language classification. Do not claim future AI features work unless implemented.

## 10. Build and delivery quality
Run the application and build before completion; fix TypeScript and runtime errors; verify major routes, authentication, database operations, complaint creation, assignment, status updates, escalation, dashboards, and responsive design. Serve `GET /manus-routes.json` with the complete page route manifest. Keep `/api/health` unauthenticated and healthy for deployment. Preserve production-safe environment handling, upload validation, rate limiting where appropriate, secure variables, friendly errors, and the configured Docker/build/publish contract.
